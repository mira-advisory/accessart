# Global Art Market Research

**Research file 01 · AccessArt build guide · Compiled 30 August 2026**

The global art market is emerging from a two-year correction: total sales rose 4% to US$59.6 billion in 2025, with growth concentrated at the very top end while transaction volumes — the number of works changing hands — continued to climb at accessible price points. The online channel has receded from its pandemic peak to US$9.2 billion (15% of the market) but remains ~50% above pre-COVID levels and is the market's primary engine for new-buyer acquisition. Structurally, the market is tilting in AccessArt's direction: sub-US$50,000 works now account for a record share of lots sold, small dealers have out-performed large ones over 2023–24, half of surveyed collectors have bought via Instagram without seeing the work in person, and gallery closures are pushing artists toward direct-to-collector channels. The main caution flag: in 2025 the lower price tiers plateaued (value and volume down ~2%) while the top end rebounded, so AccessArt is entering a high-volume, price-sensitive segment where discovery, trust tools (AR preview, returns) and affordability mechanics (rental, exchange) — not price appreciation — will drive conversion.

## 1. Market size and growth

The authoritative source is the **Art Basel & UBS Global Art Market Report 2026** by Arts Economics (Dr Clare McAndrew), published March 2026 and covering calendar 2025 ([Art Basel](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026?lang=en); [UBS report hub + full PDF](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/our-firm/art/art-market-research.html)).

- **Total global sales: US$59.6 billion in 2025, up 4% year-on-year** — the first growth after two down years (Art Basel & UBS Art Market Report 2026, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026?lang=en). For context, 2023 was US$65.0bn (–4%) and 2024 US$57.5bn (–12%) (The Art Market 2025 via Artsy, https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-5-key-takeaways-art-basel-ubss-report-the-art-market-2025). The market remains below its 2022 cyclical peak of ~US$67.8bn (The Art Newspaper, https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/03/12/art-basel-ubs-art-market-report-2025-four-percent-growth).
- **Dealer vs auction split (2025):** dealer/gallery sales rose 2% to **US$34.8bn** (~58% of the market); public auction sales rose 9% to **US$20.7bn**; private sales brokered by auction houses fell ~4–5% to **US$4.2bn** (Art Basel & UBS 2026, same URL).
- **Value down, volume up — the defining post-COVID pattern.** Transactions rose 3% to 40.5 million in 2024 even as value fell 12%, and rose a further 2% to **41.5 million in 2025** (Art Basel & UBS 2025 via Artsy, above; Art Basel & UBS 2026 via Artlyst, https://artlyst.com/art-basel-and-ubs-global-market-report-2026-highlights-growth/).
- **Geography:** the US led with US$26.0bn (44% share, +5%), the UK US$10.5bn (18%, +2%), China US$8.5bn (14%, +1%); the top three markets held 76% of global value (Art Basel & UBS 2026, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026?lang=en). Australia sits in the long tail of "rest of world" and is not separately broken out in the global report.
- **Sentiment:** 43% of dealers expected sales to improve in 2026 (up 10 points YoY) and 38% expected stability (Art Basel & UBS 2026, same URL). Dealer operating costs rose ~5% on average — above sales growth — and 56% of dealers reported negative impacts from US tariffs (The Art Newspaper, above).

## 2. The online art market

Figures below are the Arts Economics series for online sales by dealers, auction houses and online platforms (it under-weights informal artist-direct e-commerce, which is hard to measure — relevant to AccessArt's model).

| Year | Online sales | Share of total market |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 (pre-COVID) | ~US$6.0bn | 9% |
| 2020 | US$12.4bn | 25% (share peak) |
| 2021 | US$13.3bn (value peak) | 20% |
| 2023 | US$11.8bn | 18% |
| 2024 | US$10.5bn (–11%) | 18% |
| 2025 | US$9.2bn | 15% |

Sources: 2019–2021 — The Art Market 2021/2022 (via ArtfixDaily, https://www.artfixdaily.com/news_feed/2022/03/30/2508-art-market-soars-to-651-billion-in-2021-according-to-art-basel-ub); 2023 — Robb Report on The Art Market 2024, https://robbreport.com/shelter/art-collectibles/2023-art-market-report-1235550812/; 2024 — Artsy on The Art Market 2025 (above); 2025 — Art Basel & UBS 2026 (https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026-art-market-trends).

Key readings:

- **Online has receded but not reverted.** 2025's US$9.2bn is the lowest since 2019, yet still ~50% above the 2019 baseline; 2024's US$10.5bn was described in the report as 76% above pre-pandemic levels (Artsy, above). The pandemic permanently roughly doubled online's share of the market (9% → 15–18%).
- **The decline is partly a mix effect:** the 2024–25 value slump was driven by the online-heavy low end cooling and by buyers returning to fairs — art fair sales rebounded to 35% of dealer turnover in 2025, their highest since 2022 (Art Basel & UBS 2026 trends, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026-art-market-trends).
- **Online is the new-buyer channel:** ~40% of dealers' online sales in 2024 went to brand-new buyers (The Art Market 2025 via Artsy, above), and 49% of dealers' buyers overall in 2025 were new to their business (Art Basel & UBS 2026, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026?lang=en).
- Note: the dedicated **Hiscox Online Art Trade Report ended with its 2023 edition** (Hiscox pivoted to AI research), so Arts Economics is now the only consistent annual online-market series (Hiscox archive, https://www.hiscox.co.uk/online-art-trade-report/archive).

## 3. Price-tier dynamics: the lower and middle market

This is AccessArt's territory, and the evidence over 2023–25 shows a **structural shift of transaction volume toward accessible price points**, with a 2025 wrinkle worth understanding.

**2024 — the low end carried the market (The Art Market 2025):**

- At auction, the **under-US$5,000 segment was the only tier to grow, up 7% in value with 13% more lots sold**, while every higher tier declined (The Art Market 2025 auctions chapter, https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/the-art-market-2025/auctions).
- Among dealers, the smallest businesses grew fastest: turnover **<US$250k up 17%** (second straight year of growth) and **US$250k–500k up 25%**, versus **–3% for US$5–10m** and **–9% for US$10m+** dealers, 64% of whom reported lower sales (The Art Market 2025 dealers chapter, https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/dealers).

**2025 — the top end rebounded; the low end plateaued (Art Market Report 2026):**

- Auction sales of works **above US$10m rose ~30% in value** (Artlyst reports "nearly 40%" on one measure; the top 50 lots rose 30% and the top 10 rose 48%), and sales above US$1m rose ~21% with 15% more lots (Art Basel & UBS 2026, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026?lang=en; Artlyst, https://artlyst.com/art-basel-and-ubs-global-market-report-2026-highlights-growth/). November 2025 New York sales alone topped US$2bn, including Klimt's US$236.4m record (Art Basel 2025 roundup, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-market-2025-roundup?lang=en).
- **Sales below US$50,000 slipped ~2% in both value and volume in 2025** — a plateau after two strong years, not a collapse (Art Basel & UBS 2026, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026?lang=en).

**The structural picture still favours volume at the low end:**

- In the US auction market, **works under US$50,000 made up 61% of all lots sold in 2025, versus a 48% average in 2015–19** (Bank of America US Art Market Report, powered by ArtTactic, April 2026, https://www.ml.com/articles/art-market-spring-update.html).
- The number of artists trading at US auctions widened from 2,717 (2015) to 3,315 (2025) — a broader, flatter market (same BofA/ArtTactic report).
- Artprice counted **531,800 fine-art auction lots under US$1,000 in 2025, up from 488,210 in 2024** (Artprice Annual Report 2025, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/artmarketcom-publishes-its-32nd-artprice-annual-report--the--art-market-in-2025--showing-12-growth-with-the-usa-strengthening-its-dominant-position-plus-gemini-deep-thinks-ai-audit-of-artprices-strategy-for-20262030-302708675.html). (Artprice's headline "+12% growth in 2025" is its auction-only measure and is not comparable to Arts Economics' whole-market figure.)

Net read: value headlines are made at the top, but the *transaction economy* — where a marketplace earns take-rate on volume — keeps broadening at the sub-US$5k/sub-US$50k level, with 2025 a consolidation year rather than a reversal.

## 4. Buyer demographics and behaviour

The best current source is the **Art Basel & UBS Survey of Global Collecting 2025** (published autumn 2025; 3,100 HNW collectors across 10 markets, 74% of respondents millennial or Gen Z) (Art Basel, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-survey-of-global-collecting-2025; full PDF https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Survey-of-Global-Collecting-in-2025.pdf). Caveat: this surveys wealthy collectors, not mass-market consumers, but the behavioural direction is what matters for AccessArt.

- **Buying unseen via social is now mainstream: 51% of collectors who bought from a dealer made at least one purchase via Instagram *without viewing the work in person*, up from 41% in 2023** (Survey of Global Collecting 2025 via Art Basel takeaways, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-market-survey-of-global-collecting-2025-female-digital?lang=en).
- **Wealth allocation to art is rising:** average 20% of wealth in collections (up from 15%); **Gen Z allocates 26%** (same source).
- **Women are out-spending men** (46% more on art and antiques in 2024), and female-artist representation in their collections is near half (same source; ARTnews commentary, https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/art-basel-ubs-report-women-and-gen-z-1234758514/).
- **First-time buyers dominate flows:** 49% of dealers' 2025 buyers were new to that business (Art Basel & UBS 2026), and **46% of new auction bidders/buyers in 2025 were millennials or Gen Z**, with luxury/collectible categories drawing 38% of first-timers (Artlyst on the 2026 report, https://artlyst.com/art-basel-and-ubs-global-market-report-2026-highlights-growth/).
- **Motivation is shifting from investment to identity:** younger buyers report purpose-driven, emotionally led collecting tied to identity and community; Gen Z cross-collects (sneakers, handbags, sports memorabilia) and millennials lean to design/decorative arts; HNW collectors attended an average of 48 art events in 2024 (Survey takeaways, above).
- **AR closes the "can't see it" gap:** on Saatchi Art, over 70% of buyers hesitated to purchase because they couldn't see the work in advance; users of its "View in My Room" WebAR feature **spent 17% more and converted at 4× the rate** of non-users (Rock Paper Reality / 8th Wall case study, https://rockpaperreality.com/our-work/saatchi-art/; https://www.8thwall.com/rockpaperreality/saatchi-art-view-in-my-room).

## 5. Structural trends since ~2019

- **Mid-tier gallery squeeze and consolidation.** 2025 saw prominent closures — Blum (30 years), Kasmin (35), Sperone Westwater (50), Venus Over Manhattan, CLEARING — driven by costs rising faster than sales; yet reported openings (42% of activity) still outpaced closures (25%), suggesting restructuring rather than collapse. Galleries are sharing spaces and merging back-office/secondary operations (e.g. Pace Di Donna Schrader) (ARTnews, https://www.artnews.com/feature/2025-gallery-closures-collapse-transition-smarter-market-1234765872/; Art Basel roundup, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-market-2025-roundup?lang=en; The Art Newspaper comment, https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/12/16/comment-after-a-market-shake-up-in-2025-its-time-to-create-a-right-sized-art-trade).
- **Direct-from-artist sales are growing.** With fewer gallery slots, artists increasingly sell via Instagram, TikTok and their own sites, keeping roughly 70–90% of the price versus the standard ~50% gallery split (Artsy, https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-artists-bypassing-dealers-selling-directly-collectors; https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-artists-jump-started-careers-selling-directly-collectors-instagram). No reliable global dollar figure exists for this channel — it largely sits outside the Arts Economics data.
- **NFT boom fully bust, digital behaviour persists.** Art-NFT trading collapsed ~93% from US$2.9bn (2021) to US$197m (2024) and just US$23.8m in Q1 2025; platforms like MakersPlace and KnownOrigin shut down (DappRadar, https://dappradar.com/blog/nft-arts-shocking-collapse-from-2-9-billion-boom-to-23-8-million-bust-what-went-wrong). Yet over half of HNW collectors bought *some* digital work in 2025 (Survey of Global Collecting 2025, above) — the digital-native buying habit outlived the speculation.
- **AR/"view on your wall" is proven, cheap and expected** — WebAR (no app install) is now standard on Saatchi Art's 1.4m-work catalogue with the conversion economics cited in §4 (Rock Paper Reality, above).
- **Representation is broadening:** female artists reached 50% of rosters at primary-market galleries in 2025, and 37% of dealer sales by value (vs 28% in 2018) (Art Basel & UBS 2026, https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026?lang=en).
- **Fairs and events rebounded** (35% of dealer turnover), confirming buyers want physical touchpoints alongside digital discovery — an argument for pop-up/partner activations, not pure-play online (Art Basel & UBS 2026 trends, above).

## 6. Key takeaways for AccessArt

- **Ride the volume, not the value.** Headline growth is top-end-driven, but transactions hit 41.5m in 2025 and sub-US$50k works are a record 61% of US lots sold — AccessArt's segment is where nearly all *transactions* happen, and small sellers out-grew large ones in 2023–24.
- **Online is smaller but stickier than the hype cycle suggests:** ~US$9–10bn/year, 15–18% of the market, roughly double its pre-COVID share — and the dominant channel for first-time buyers (~40% of online sales to new buyers). Build for acquisition economics, not just GMV.
- **Price the 2025 wrinkle in:** the sub-US$50k tier dipped ~2% in 2025, so growth must come from expanding the buyer pool (renters, first-timers, exchangers), not from assuming a rising low-end tide.
- **Social-first discovery is table stakes:** 51% of surveyed collectors have bought via Instagram sight-unseen; 46% of new auction buyers are millennial/Gen Z. Instagram/TikTok-native artist storytelling should be the top of AccessArt's funnel.
- **Ship AR "view on your wall" at launch:** 4× conversion and +17% basket on Saatchi Art, directly attacking the #1 stated barrier (70% hesitate because they can't see the work in situ).
- **The artist-supply pitch writes itself:** mid-tier gallery closures and 70–90% direct-sale economics (vs ~50% gallery splits) give emerging artists strong reasons to join a low-friction marketplace that handles logistics, payments and audience.
- **Rental/exchange is differentiated access, not a market norm:** no incumbent report tracks consumer art rental — AccessArt can position rent/swap as the affordability and "try before you buy" mechanism the unseen-purchase barrier demands, while avoiding NFT-style speculative framing (physical originals, real provenance).
- **Data gap to fill locally:** the global reports don't break out Australia; a dedicated Australian/SEQ market sizing (research file to follow) is needed before financial modelling.
