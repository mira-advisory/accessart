# 09 — Creative Web Interactions: hover-driven patterns for the AccessArt landing page

*Research date: 2026-08-30. Stack context: React 18 + Vite + plain CSS. Aesthetic context: post-grunge/zine — dirty paper, ink black, one loud red, Anton caps vs Instrument Serif italics, grain, tape, stamps, marquees.*

**Executive summary.** The current award-site vocabulary is overwhelmingly hover-driven: cursor-following image reveals on list/nav hover, full-section colour floods and inversions, kinetic and variable typography (roll, scramble, weight-shift), custom cursors and image trails, magnetic buttons, and velocity-reactive marquees — almost all built on GSAP (+ ScrollTrigger, SplitText) with Lenis for smooth scroll, or Framer Motion in React shops. Brutalist/zine sites (streetwear drops, record labels, MSCHF-style chaos) use the *same* techniques but with deliberately rougher easing, scattered/draggable objects and glitch, which is exactly the register AccessArt wants: cool, not corporate-slick. The UX evidence says spectacle works when it *explains* (hover as "preview-on-demand") and fails when it hides critical info behind hover (invisible on touch), loops endlessly, or competes with the single CTA; `prefers-reduced-motion` and `@media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)` gating are non-negotiable. For AccessArt the highest-value move is the portfolio-list pattern applied to the three doors: **hover a door, real artworks bloom under the cursor** — because art is the product and hover-reveal makes the product the payoff of every interaction. A ranked 11-item shortlist with effort ratings and touch fallbacks closes the doc.

## 1. The 2024–2026 award-site interaction vocabulary

These are the patterns that recur across Awwwards SOTD/Honorable Mentions, FWA winners and studio portfolios. Awwwards maintains whole curated collections of them — ["Hovers, Cursors and Cute Interactions"](https://www.awwwards.com/awwwards/collections/hovers-cursors-and-cute-interactions/) and ["Filters and Effects"](https://www.awwwards.com/awwwards/collections/filters-and-effects/) — which is itself a signal of how central hover has become.

### 1.1 Cursor-following image reveal on list/nav hover ("the portfolio-list pattern")
The signature move of the last five years: a text list (projects, menu items, artists) where hovering a row makes an image float up and follow the cursor. Awwwards inspiration entries include ["Image Reveal Hover — Crafted"](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/image-reveal-hover-crafted), ["Cursor Follow Background Reveal"](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/cursor-follow-background-reveal-renflow-designs) and ["Hover Trails and Mask Reveal on Cursor Move"](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/hover-trails-and-mask-reveal-on-cursor-move-marga-navarro). The canonical build tutorial is Codrops' ["Creating a Menu Image Animation on Hover"](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2020/07/01/creating-a-menu-image-animation-on-hover/): track `mousemove`, lerp the image's translate toward the pointer each frame (GSAP `quickTo` or a small rAF loop), fade/clip it in on `mouseenter`. **Implementation:** JS required (pointer position); GSAP is standard but ~40 lines of vanilla rAF + CSS transforms works fine.

### 1.2 Full-section colour floods / inversions on hover
Hovering a link or section swaps the whole background — black→red, paper→ink — often with a circular clip-path expanding from the cursor. Seen constantly on brutalist-leaning SOTDs (e.g. Awwwards' ["Hover Effects and Background Pattern Animation"](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/hover-effects-and-background-pattern-animation), tagged brutalism). **Implementation:** pure CSS for simple floods (`transition: background-color, color`), or CSS custom properties set from JS (`--x`, `--y`) driving `clip-path: circle(0 at var(--x) var(--y))` for the from-cursor version. No library needed.

### 1.3 Kinetic / variable typography
Three recurring sub-patterns, all covered in [Digital Silk's kinetic typography overview](https://www.digitalsilk.com/web-design/web-trends/kinetic-typography/) and [Studio Meyer's GSAP examples piece](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/kinetische-typografie):
- **Text roll** — link text duplicated, both copies translate vertically on hover so the word "rolls" ([CodeMyUI split-word roll demo](https://codemyui.com/split-word-roll-effect-hover/); [SplitText link-hover cloneable](https://www.flowradar.com/cloneables/splittext-js-link-hover-text-animation)). Pure CSS if you pre-duplicate the text in markup; SplitText/split-type if you want per-letter stagger.
- **Scramble/decode** — characters cycle through glyphs before settling; GSAP's ScrambleText plugin (free since GSAP went 100% free under Webflow in 2024) or ~30 lines of vanilla JS.
- **Variable-font weight shift** — headings animate `font-variation-settings` weight/width on hover; noted by Digital Silk as one of the most accessible kinetic patterns to implement. Pure CSS `transition: font-variation-settings`. (Note: **Anton is single-weight**, so for AccessArt this applies to a secondary face or via `font-stretch`/scale fakery.)
- Codrops' ["From SplitText to MorphSVG: 5 creative demos using free GSAP plugins"](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/05/14/from-splittext-to-morphsvg-5-creative-demos-using-free-gsap-plugins/) is the best current index of text-effect building blocks.

### 1.4 Custom cursors and cursor trails
A replacement cursor (dot/ring/blob) that scales over links, morphs into "VIEW"/"DRAG" labels, or blends via `mix-blend-mode: difference`. [Dennis Snellenberg's portfolio](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/dennis-snellenberg) (Awwwards SOTD, 2× Developer Award, Independent of the Year nominee) is the most-cloned example — Olivier Larose maintains full rebuild tutorials of it ([awwwards landing page rebuild](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/awwwards-landing-page), [sticky cursor](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/sticky-cursor)). Cuberto's gooey cursor and Locomotive's site popularised the genre (both agency sites; verify current state before citing to the founder — agency sites redesign often). Motion (Framer Motion's successor) now ships a first-class [Cursor component](https://motion.dev/docs/cursor) with [magnetic snapping](https://motion.dev/magazine/introducing-magnetic-cursors-in-motion-cursor). **Implementation:** JS pointer tracking + lerp; hide native cursor only on `(pointer:fine)` devices.

### 1.5 Magnetic buttons
Buttons that pull toward the cursor inside a proximity radius, elastic-return on leave. Popularised by Snellenberg and countless SOTDs; [Olivier Larose documents both GSAP (`quickTo`) and Framer Motion (spring) builds](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/magnetic-button), and there's an [open-source remake of the Awwwards magnetic buttons](https://github.com/waleed46shah/project_MagneticButtons). **Implementation:** ~25 lines of JS; GSAP's elastic ease is the reason people use GSAP here.

### 1.6 Velocity-reactive marquees
Infinite marquees that speed up, reverse, or skew based on scroll velocity. GreenSock's own [skew-on-scroll-velocity CodePen](https://codepen.io/GreenSock/pen/eYpGLYL) shows the `self.getVelocity()` ScrollTrigger technique; typically paired with [Lenis smooth scroll feeding GSAP's ticker](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/28/the-never-ending-story-building-a-seamless-infinite-scroll-experience-with-gsap-lenis/) so velocity is smooth. **Implementation:** base marquee is pure CSS (`@keyframes translateX`); velocity reaction needs JS (ScrollTrigger or a hand-rolled scroll-delta tracker writing to a CSS var for `animation-duration`/`transform: skewX()`).

### 1.7 Image trails following the mouse
A stream of images spawning along the cursor path and decaying — pure "art everywhere" energy. Codrops' [Image Trail Effects](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/08/07/image-trail-effects/) (multiple variants) is the classic; the 2026 update is the [gravity-based mouse trail](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/20/made-with-gsap-building-a-fun-gravity-based-mouse-trail/) where spawned images fall and bounce. **Implementation:** GSAP + a pool of absolutely-positioned `<img>`s; needs throttling by pointer-distance, not time.

### 1.8 Scroll-triggered pinning
Sections that pin while content scrubs through (horizontal galleries, step-by-step "how it works"). GSAP ScrollTrigger `pin: true` is the industry default ([60+ ScrollTrigger examples](https://freefrontend.com/scroll-trigger-js/)); simple progress-linked effects can now be **CSS-only** via [scroll-driven animations](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Scroll-driven_animations) (`animation-timeline: view()` / `scroll()`), at [~85% support — everywhere except stable Firefox, where it's behind a flag](https://dev.to/softheartengineer/mastering-css-scroll-timeline-a-complete-guide-to-animation-on-scroll-in-2025-3g7p); [Chrome now also has scroll-*triggered* animations](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/scroll-triggered-animations). Keep the no-support fallback state visible.

**Tooling consensus:** GSAP (+ScrollTrigger/SplitText, all free now) for timeline-y, easing-critical work; Lenis (from darkroom.engineering/Studio Freight) for smooth scroll ([integration guide](https://www.jiapixel.com/blogs/lenis-smooth-scrolling-elevate-your-web-animations-with-buttery-smoothness)); Framer Motion where the site is React-first; split-type as the free SplitText alternative (less needed now SplitText is free).

## 2. Grunge / zine / brutalist references — what makes it cool instead of corporate

The brutalist lineage runs straight from 70s–80s punk zines (Sniffin' Glue, Search & Destroy) to today's raw web — [Made Good's brutalist graphic design explainer](https://madegooddesigns.com/brutalist-graphic-design/) draws that line explicitly, and [Kittl's brutalism guide](https://www.kittl.com/blogs/brutalist-design-art-stl/) covers the same DNA. Key working references:

- **Balenciaga** — spartan, mono-spaced, industrial; proof a fashion brand can be brutalist-quiet rather than animated at all ([DesignMantic's brutalism guide](https://www.designmantic.com/blog/brutalism-in-web-design/)). Restraint is itself a move.
- **MSCHF** ([mschf.xyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSCHF)) — the drop-site archetype: numbered drops, chaotic grid of dissimilar artefacts, countdowns; the interaction *is* the product taxonomy ([brand analysis](https://nufero.com/how-mschf-turned-chaos-into-currency-lessons-in-provocative-brand-building/), [design gallery entry](https://maxibestof.one/websites/46883-mschf)). Lesson: irreverent structure > polished motion.
- **Record labels** — Stones Throw ([store](https://www.stonesthrow.com/store/)) and Sacred Bones keep dense, catalogue-first, functional-ugly layouts; [One Page Love's brutalist collection](https://onepagelove.com/brutalist-websites) catalogues more, including label site Acte's floating raw blocks ([Obriy's brutalism feature](https://www.obriy.design/post/web-brutalism-key-features-and-5-creative-examples)). *Streetwear labels like Brain Dead and Online Ceramics are frequently cited in this space but their storefronts change seasonally — treat specific interaction claims about them as unverified until visited.* **Heaven by Marc Jacobs** built its whole brand on emo-grunge/2000s-internet nostalgia ([USF MediaZine analysis](https://usfblogs.usfca.edu/mediastudied/2022/12/09/marc-jacobs-heaven-saves-gen-z-from-the-pits-of-fast-fashion-hell/)), though its commerce pages now live inside marcjacobs.com's conventional shell — the zine energy lives in campaigns, not the cart.
- **The 2025–26 trend reports agree the wind is behind this aesthetic**: grunge returns "with controlled texture… a reaction to sameness" and as a counter to AI-smooth design ([Envato grunge design guide](https://elements.envato.com/learn/grunge-design)); scrapbook/collage layouts with torn edges and layered textures are a named web trend ([Designmodo on scrapbook web design](https://designmodo.com/scrapbook-website-design/), [Skillshare on the scrapbook aesthetic](https://www.skillshare.com/en/blog/cut-paste-post-why-the-scrapbook-aesthetic-is-sticking-around/)); one survey claims 42% of top-500 sites carried neo-brutalist elements by Jan 2024 with strong Gen-Z affinity ([Marketing Scoop](https://www.marketingscoop.com/website/neo-brutalism-your-comprehensive-guide-to-the-web-design-trend-dominating-2024/) — self-reported stat, treat as directional).

**Interactions that read zine, not corporate:**
- **Scattered/draggable polaroids & stickers** — Codrops' [Scattered Polaroids Gallery](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2014/01/28/scattered-polaroids-gallery/) is the ancestor; the modern version is a freeform draggable canvas with random rotation, drop-shadow-on-drag, hover-to-straighten ([drag-and-drop polaroid gallery how-to](https://www.bpwebdesign.co.za/how-to-create-a-drag-and-drop-polaroid-style-image-gallery/), [Framer Scatter Gallery component](https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/components/scatter-gallery/) as a commercial articulation of the same idea).
- **Chaotic cursor photo-scatter** — photos flashing at random spots as the cursor moves, "chaotic by design", noted as a brutalist signature by [Obriy](https://www.obriy.design/post/web-brutalism-key-features-and-5-creative-examples).
- **Glitch/xerox hover** — RGB-split, slice-jitter, threshold/photocopy filter flicker on hover; dozens of CSS-only recipes exist ([Effect.Labs' 56 CSS text animations incl. glitch](https://effect-labs.com/en/pages/text.html)).
- **Rubber-stamp / spray-marker cursor marks** — cursor leaves stamped marks or marker strokes on the paper (canvas-based); rarer, high-delight, fits the "stamps" motif AccessArt already uses.
- The unifying principle from [Obriy](https://www.obriy.design/post/web-brutalism-key-features-and-5-creative-examples): a slightly "unfinished" surface *invites* poking. Rough easing (steps(), snap, overshoot), duct-tape z-index layering and hand-placed rotation make the same GSAP techniques feel punk instead of fintech.

## 3. What converts vs what confuses

- **One page, one goal.** High-converting landing pages share a clear headline, a *single* action-oriented CTA and strong hierarchy; too many choices kill decisions ([FormAssembly's analysis of 20 converting pages](https://www.formassembly.com/blog/20-converting-landing-pages/), [Eminence's 2026 landing-page guide](https://eminence.ch/en/create-successful-landing-page-increase-conversion-rate/)). Unbounce's benchmark median across 57M conversions is ~6.6% — spectacle is worth it only if it feeds that one action.
- **Animation helps when it explains, hurts when it loops.** Motion increases understanding (Wyzowl's 74% comprehension figure, cited via [Boagworld's CTA piece](https://boagworld.com/design/call-to-action/)), but continuously looping animation demonstrably distracts from messaging — subtle, intentional, attention-*guiding* motion is the recommendation ([Boagworld](https://boagworld.com/design/call-to-action/), [Metaweb on high-converting pages](https://metaweb.com.au/blog/science-behind-high-converting-landing-pages/)). For AccessArt: **hover = explanation-on-demand** (hover a door → see actual art; hover a step → see the mechanic). Idle-state motion should be near-zero apart from the marquee.
- **Hover-only information is invisible on touch.** Touchscreens have no hover state; hover must never be the *only* route to important content or functionality ([UXPin on touch design](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/responsive-design-touch-devices-key-considerations/), [UX Pickle on hover alternatives](https://uxpickle.com/alternatives-to-hover-interaction-on-touchscreens/)). NN/g adds a timing warning: content revealed instantly on mouseover causes accidental activations; a click/tap is the clearest intent signal ([NN/g timing guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/timing-exposing-content/)). Rule for AccessArt: hover reveals are *garnish* — the doors' labels, copy and CTAs must be fully legible with zero hover.
- **Motion sickness is a real failure mode.** Scaling/panning large objects, parallax and cursor-driven drift are documented vestibular triggers ([MDN on `prefers-reduced-motion`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion), [web.dev on motion](https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/motion)). WCAG 2.3.3 requires interaction-triggered motion be disableable unless essential ([W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). Best practice: honour the OS setting via the media query, disable the *large* movers (image trails, floods, parallax) and keep tiny fades ([Think Company on reduced motion](https://www.thinkcompany.com/blog/leverage-reduced-motion-for-more-accessible-web-animation/), [a11y with Lindsey](https://www.a11ywithlindsey.com/blog/reducing-motion-improve-accessibility/)).
- Also mundane but real: heavy animation JS hurts LCP/INP on the page where first impressions are made ([DebugBear on landing page performance](https://www.debugbear.com/blog/improving-landing-page-performance)). Prefer CSS, lazy-load the fancy layer.

## 4. Implementation notes for React 18 + Vite + plain CSS

**CSS-only (zero bytes of JS):**
- Colour floods/inversions, tape "lift" on hover, sticker straighten/rotate, text roll (pre-duplicated markup), variable-font transitions, glitch via layered `clip-path`/`text-shadow` keyframes, base marquee, image zoom/filter swaps (grayscale→colour, `filter: contrast(2) grayscale(1)` xerox mode).
- Custom cursor *image* via `cursor: url(stamp.svg) 8 8, auto` (32×32 limit practically; static only).
- Scroll-driven reveals via `animation-timeline: view()` — [not yet in stable Firefox](https://dev.to/softheartengineer/mastering-css-scroll-timeline-a-complete-guide-to-animation-on-scroll-in-2025-3g7p), so author the keyframe so the un-animated state is the *final* legible state (progressive enhancement), per [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Scroll-driven_animations).

**Needs JS but no library (rAF + CSS variables, ~20–60 lines each):**
- Cursor-following reveal (lerp `--x/--y`, `transform: translate3d`), magnetic buttons, custom cursor dot, from-cursor clip-path flood, scramble text, velocity-reactive marquee (scroll delta → CSS var). This is the sweet spot for a plain-CSS stack: one small `useLerpPointer` hook + CSS vars covers doors, buttons and cursor.

**When to buy a library:**
- **GSAP**: core ~23–27KB gzip, modular; all plugins (ScrollTrigger, SplitText, ScrambleText, Draggable) now free ([pkgpulse comparison](https://www.pkgpulse.com/compare/framer-motion-vs-gsap), [hontran.dev verdict](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/gsap-vs-framer-motion)). Worth it the moment you want pinning, elastic/inertia easing, or Draggable with momentum.
- **Framer Motion (Motion)**: ~30–32KB full, or **~4.6KB via `LazyMotion` + `m`** ([same comparisons](https://www.pkgpulse.com/compare/framer-motion-vs-gsap)) — plus the ready-made [Cursor component with magnetic behaviour](https://motion.dev/docs/cursor). More idiomatic in React (props, `AnimatePresence`), weaker at scroll pinning.
- **Lenis**: ~3–4KB, only if smooth scroll is wanted; wire into GSAP's ticker to avoid jitter ([Codrops GSAP+Lenis guide](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/28/the-never-ending-story-building-a-seamless-infinite-scroll-experience-with-gsap-lenis/)). Honest note: smooth-scroll hijacking is the most "corporate-slick 2022" item here and arguably *against* the zine register — skip it first.
- Recommendation for AccessArt: **vanilla hooks + CSS vars first; add GSAP core+ScrollTrigger only if pinning/inertia makes the cut.** Avoid loading both GSAP and Framer Motion.

**Pointer-events & input-gating tricks:**
- Floating reveal images and custom cursors get `pointer-events: none` so they never eat clicks; the hovered list keeps hit-testing simple.
- Gate all hover/cursor code behind `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` (CSS) and `matchMedia` (JS) — the recommended combo per [Smashing's hover/pointer media query guide](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2022/03/guide-hover-pointer-media-queries/) and [CSS-Tricks' caveats](https://css-tricks.com/interaction-media-features-and-their-potential-for-incorrect-assumptions/) (hybrid devices exist; `any-hover` ≠ primary input). Never `document.ontouchstart` sniffing.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: one global gate. In CSS, wrap large-motion rules in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`; in JS hooks, early-return from rAF loops when `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches`. Replace motion with opacity/underline state changes, don't remove feedback ([web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/motion), [MDN accessibility media queries](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Media_queries/Using_for_accessibility)).
- **Touch fallbacks pattern**: every hover reveal has (a) a static designed touch state (e.g. the door already shows one taped artwork), or (b) a tap equivalent (first tap = reveal, second = navigate is *fragile*; prefer always-visible-on-touch), or (c) a scroll-triggered version (IntersectionObserver fires the "hover" state as the section enters view on coarse pointers).

## 5. Ranked shortlist for AccessArt

Ranked by (impact on "you hover and it changes" × fit to zine aesthetic ÷ effort). Effort: S ≈ ≤half-day, M ≈ 1–2 days, L ≈ 3+ days.

| # | Effect | Section | Effort |
|---|--------|---------|--------|
| 1 | Cursor-following artwork reveal on the doors | Three-door hero | M |
| 2 | Ink flood + invert on door hover | Three-door hero | S |
| 3 | Taped-photo lift & straighten | Café wall | S |
| 4 | Stamp cursor with "ENTER / DRAG / SEND IT" states | Global | M |
| 5 | Velocity-reactive marquee | Marquee strip | S–M |
| 6 | Scramble-settle headlines | Hero + section titles | S |
| 7 | Draggable sticker scatter | Café wall / footer | M |
| 8 | Magnetic, mis-registered CTA | Final CTA | S |
| 9 | Hover-xerox panels (grayscale→colour) | Panels / how-it-goes | S |
| 10 | Image trail of artworks | Pull-quote or 404/footer only | M |
| 11 | Scroll-pinned "how it goes" stamps | How-it-goes | L |

1. **Cursor-following artwork reveal on the three doors** *(the priority — art is the product).* Hovering a door makes real artworks bloom under the cursor and drift with it — lerped `--x/--y` + `pointer-events:none` images, cycling through 3–5 pieces per door (à la [Codrops menu-image-animation](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2020/07/01/creating-a-menu-image-animation-on-hover/)). Fits because the reveal is *content*, not decoration: the site literally answers "what's behind this door?" with paintings. Give images torn edges/tape corners so the reveal reads zine, not lightbox. **Effort M. Touch fallback:** each door permanently shows one taped artwork that swaps on an IntersectionObserver-driven interval when the hero is in view.
2. **Ink flood + invert on door hover.** The hovered door floods to ink black (or the loud red), its Anton label inverting to paper; siblings dim to 40%. From-cursor `clip-path: circle()` if feeling fancy, plain background transition if not. Cheap, huge perceived interactivity, and colour-blocking is native brutalist grammar. **Effort S. Touch fallback:** `:active`/tap applies the flood for 300ms before navigation.
3. **Taped-photo lift & straighten (café wall).** Each artwork sits at random rotation under tape; hover lifts it (scale 1.04, shadow, rotate→0) as if peeling off the wall, with a stamped price/artist caption appearing. Pure CSS. Straight out of the scattered-polaroids lineage ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2014/01/28/scattered-polaroids-gallery/)). **Effort S. Touch fallback:** captions permanently visible on coarse pointers via `@media (hover:none)`.
4. **Stamp cursor with states.** On fine pointers, a small red rubber-stamp ring replaces the cursor; over doors it becomes a stamped "ENTER", over the wall "PEEL", over the CTA "SEND IT" — dry lowercase, ironic. Lerp-follow dot + label swap; `mix-blend-mode: difference` over grain. **Effort M. Touch fallback:** none needed — feature simply doesn't mount off `(pointer:fine)`.
5. **Velocity-reactive marquee.** The existing marquee speeds up and skews with scroll velocity ([GreenSock technique](https://codepen.io/GreenSock/pen/eYpGLYL)) and pauses/reverses on hover with the hovered word turning red. Makes an already-zine element feel alive. **Effort S (hover) to M (velocity). Touch fallback:** velocity reaction works from scroll, no hover needed; keep base CSS animation as floor.
6. **Scramble-settle headlines.** Anton headlines decode from punctuation-glyph noise on first view; on hover a single word re-scrambles. Vanilla ~30 lines or GSAP ScrambleText. Reads as photocopier/teletype, not sci-fi, if the glyph set is `/#×@*` and duration <400ms. **Effort S. Touch fallback:** view-triggered version runs on scroll for everyone; hover re-trigger is bonus.
7. **Draggable sticker scatter.** A handful of stickers/stamps ("no beige", "real art, real walls", price tags) scattered over the café-wall section can be dragged and tossed; slight inertia, z-index pops on grab. GSAP Draggable (free) or Pointer Events + momentum. The single most "poke the zine" feature ([scatter-gallery genre](https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/components/scatter-gallery/)). **Effort M. Touch fallback:** drag works natively on touch — one of the few effects that does; just make stickers ≥44px.
8. **Magnetic, mis-registered CTA.** Final CTA button pulls toward the cursor ([Larose's GSAP/Framer builds](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/magnetic-button)) but with punk detailing: its red offset-print shadow layer lags behind the pull, snapping back with overshoot. **Effort S. Touch fallback:** `:active` squash + shadow snap on tap.
9. **Hover-xerox panels.** The value-prop panels idle as high-contrast photocopy (`filter: grayscale(1) contrast(1.6)` + grain); hover restores full colour and the Instrument Serif italic annotation "scribbles" in via animated `stroke-dashoffset` underline. Pure CSS. **Effort S. Touch fallback:** colour state applied when panel is ≥60% in view (IntersectionObserver).
10. **Image trail on the pull-quote.** Moving the mouse across the pull-quote section leaves a decaying trail of tiny artwork scraps ([Codrops image trails](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/08/07/image-trail-effects/)). Delightful but the most motion-heavy item — confine to one section, hard-disable under reduced-motion. **Effort M. Touch fallback:** none; section is fully complete without it.
11. **Scroll-pinned "how it goes".** Pin the section; each step gets slammed with a rubber-stamp animation as scroll progresses (GSAP ScrollTrigger `pin`). Highest effort, and pinning is the pattern most likely to read corporate-slick and to annoy on mobile — do last, or replace with simple `view()`-timeline stamp reveals (CSS-only). **Effort L. Touch fallback:** non-pinned stacked layout with view-triggered stamps.

**Cross-cutting rules:** every effect behind `(hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)` where hover-dependent; every large mover behind `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; no effect may carry information that isn't also present statically; the page keeps exactly one primary CTA and the doors as the only other click targets above the fold.
